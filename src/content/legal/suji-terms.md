## Your account

An account exists so that your progress survives a lost device and so that a
teacher you connect to can see the work you share. You can delete it from inside
the app; deletion removes your rows on the backend and wipes the local database.

Accounts used to abuse the service or other people may be suspended.

## What you create

Your study sets, notes, handwriting, messages and uploads stay yours. You grant
only what is needed to run the service: storing that material, syncing it
between your devices, and showing it to a teacher you have connected to.

## Teachers and students

A teacher and a student agree between themselves what they are doing and on what
terms. The app carries the lessons, the homework and the messages; it is not a
party to that arrangement, does not vet anyone, and takes no position on what is
owed between them.

Access is limited by the connection: a teacher sees the progress and submissions
of their own students and nothing else.

## Material you bring in

You can import from a photo, a PDF, an Anki deck, pasted text or a link. What you
import is your responsibility — that includes having the right to use it.
Imported material is processed to make study sets and exercises, and it is
treated as your content, not as part of the app's reference library.

## AI features

Some explanations, corrections and conversation practice come from a language
model. They are study aids, not authority. A model can be confidently wrong, and
the bundled dictionary and grammar reference are the sources to check against.

## Sources and attribution

The reference content is assembled from open datasets, and their licences require
that they be named.

**Dictionaries and characters.** JMdict, JMnedict and KANJIDIC2 come from the
Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group and are used under
CC BY-SA. Stroke-order data is KanjiVG, also CC BY-SA. Example sentences come
from Tatoeba under CC BY.

**Software.** Japanese text is segmented with Sudachi, licensed under Apache 2.0.
Spaced repetition uses FSRS, an open scheduler.

The app's licence screen carries the full list, including the component,
radical, pitch-accent and JLPT datasets, and states which material was generated
rather than sourced.
